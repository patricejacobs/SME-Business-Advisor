# Guyana SME finance and support ecosystem

last_verified: 2026-07-31

**Confidence tags used throughout:** **[V]** verified from a primary source · **[M]** single credible
secondary source · **[U]** uncertain, conflicting, or stale.

**Always tell the client to confirm rates, fees, thresholds and programme terms directly with the
institution before acting.** Section 10 lists what could not be verified.

## 0. Corrections to common misconceptions

State these when a client or another adviser repeats them:

1. **There is no verifiable standalone "Interest Rate Subsidy Programme" at the SBB.** The 6% rate
   under the Credit Guarantee Scheme appears to *be* the subsidy, delivered via participating banks.
2. **The Guyana Development Bank Act 2026 has been passed and assented to (as of 30 Jul 2026) but
   the Bank is not yet operational.** This corrects the earlier position that the bill hadn't been
   debated — see §2 for the update. Still do not tell a client the G$3m interest-free facility is
   available to draw down now; the institution itself is not yet standing up loans.
3. **The Local Content First Schedule has one target column, "End of 2022."** There is no phased
   multi-year ramp.
4. **The official exchange rate does not clear the market.** Official G$208.50/US$1; cambio
   G$222–225. **A plan costing imports at the official rate understates them by 6.5–8%.**
5. **Stripe and PayPal do not work for Guyanese merchants.** See `guyana-operating-context.md`.
6. **Republic Bank did not acquire Scotiabank Guyana.** The sale was blocked. Scotiabank remains,
   restructured as a local subsidiary from 1 Nov 2025. First Citizens does not operate in Guyana.

## 1. Small Business Bureau (SBB)

**[V]** Semi-autonomous agency under the Small Business Act 2004, operating under MINTIC.
Lot 1, La Penitence, Georgetown · (592) 226-8120/23/25/33/43 · info@sbb.gov.gy · funding@sbb.gov.gy

### Definition of a small business — the 2021 change

**[V]** The Small Business (Amendment) Act 2021 raised the test from *two of three* to **all three**:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Employees | not more than **25** |
| Gross annual revenue | not more than **G$60 million** (≈US$287k) |
| Total business assets | not more than **G$20 million** (≈US$96k) |

SBB's own FAQ now states "satisfies all three." **These are nominal 2004 figures, never indexed** —
a growing share of firms that are economically small in 2026 terms now fail the revenue test.

### Registration — free

**[V] First-time registration costs nothing.** Required: SBB registration form; National ID or
passport; GRA TIN certificate; **GRA Compliance Certificate**; NIS card and compliance; DCRA business
registration; plus Food Handler's Certificate or operational licence where applicable. Applicants
aged 60+ provide an NIS non-liability letter instead.

Prerequisite chain: DCRA registration → GRA TIN → VAT if applicable → NIS employer registration →
local council business licence → sector licences. **No turnaround time is published.**

### Small Business Development Grant

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Amount | **G$200,000 – G$500,000** **[V]** from MINTIC; may be stale |
| Frequency | **One grant per person, lifetime** **[V]** |
| Eligible | Unemployed, or employed by private sector/NGO; owner or prospective entrepreneur |
| **Ineligible** | **Employees of any government-affiliated body** — ministries, semi-autonomous agencies, RDCs, government-owned entities. SBB is explicit that any organisation with "some government control" counts |
| Documents | Business registration; ID; TIN; **complete business plan**; **quotations for items to be purchased**; valid GRA and NIS compliance. Pre-startups: 6- or 12-month cash-flow projections |
| Process | Evaluation → approval → grant agreement → disbursement → **receipts must be submitted** → monitoring visits |
| Turnaround | **Not published.** A ~2-year backlog was reported in 2023 |

**Green Business Technology Fund [V]** — historically **G$1,000,000** per award. Scoring: Innovation
& Technology 25%, Feasibility 20%, Environmental Sustainability 20%, Financial Viability 20%,
Strategic Planning 15%. 2025 cycle closed 31 Aug 2025; a 2026 cycle is likely but unconfirmed.

### Credit Guarantee Scheme — the most useful SBB product

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Maximum loan guaranteed | **G$30,000,000** |
| Coverage | **up to 40% of collateral** first-time; **up to 70%** repeat |
| Participating banks | **Republic Bank and GBTI only** — they approve and disburse, not SBB |
| Interest rate | **6%** **[M]** — on the SBB homepage but omitted from the loans page. Confirm |
| Eligibility | Business owner seeking **expansion**; registered SBB client |
| Documents | Registration; ID; TIN; business plan if startup or trading under 12 months; quotations; asset valuation; GRA and NIS compliance; **CreditInfo credit report** |

**⚠ Read the wording carefully: this is *collateral* coverage — SBB stands in for security the
borrower lacks. It is not stated as a percentage of loss on default.** Do not describe it as a
40/70% loss guarantee without confirming.

Nine sequential steps across two institutions including a site visit. **No turnaround published;
assume weeks to months.**

### SBB performance

**2025 [V]:** G$304,162,000 distributed · 3,234 entrepreneurs trained · Green Technology Grants 10
recipients · Loan Guarantee Programme 32 recipients · Disability Loan Programme 32 persons.

**The 20% government procurement set-aside [V]** — s.11(1) obliged government to use "best
endeavours" to source 20% from small businesses. The 2021 Amendment made it **mandatory and added
works (construction)**. **⚠ No published data quantifies actual attainment for any year.**

## 2. Guyana Development Bank — Act assented, not yet operational

**Update, 31 Jul 2026 [M — single news report, not yet cross-checked against the Act's official
text]:** the Guyana Development Bank Act 2026 passed the National Assembly on 27 Jul 2026 and was
assented to on 30 Jul 2026, establishing the Bank's legal, institutional and administrative
framework. This supersedes the earlier "bill tabled, not debated" position. Assent clears the way
for implementation — it does **not** mean the Bank is open for business yet; the government is
still expected to advance the institutional and administrative arrangements needed before it
starts operating.

**When citing this to a client, state the institutional facts only — do not name the President,
any Minister, or any other individual office-holder associated with the bill.** Say "the Act has
been assented to" or "the government," not who signed or piloted it.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Legislation | **Guyana Development Bank Act 2026 — passed 27 Jul 2026, assented 30 Jul 2026** |
| Capitalisation | **US$100m** in Budget 2026, first tranche of a planned **US$200m** |
| Micro tier | up to **G$3m, zero interest, zero collateral** **[M]**, reported for the general SME facility |
| SME tier | up to **G$10m** total, ~G$7m co-financed with banks at preferential rates — **not directly
  confirmed against the assented Act text; treat as the pre-assent figure until re-checked** |
| Eligibility | **Registered business + TIN required** (per the pre-assent framework — re-confirm) |
| Technical/advisory scope | Reported to include help preparing business plans, improving financial
  management, and strengthening general business capacity — aimed at eventually qualifying
  recipients for financing through the formal banking system |
| **Status 31 Jul 2026** | **Act assented, Bank NOT YET OPERATIONAL.** No launch date confirmed. A
  Q1 2026 operational target was already missed once before this Act passed |

**Two implications, still valid.** The registration + TIN requirement means the GDB **structurally
cannot reach informal traders** — IPED's core clientele. And a zero-interest G$3m facility would
undercut SBB's 6% guaranteed loan below G$3m, leaving SBB's advantage in the **G$3m–G$30m band**.

**Do not promise a client that G$3m interest-free financing is available to draw down now.** It is
reasonable to tell a client the Bank has legally been established and to watch for it becoming
operational, but not to plan a funding ask around it until it is actually lending.

### Basic eligibility checklist [M — from an outreach/promotional graphic, not yet cross-checked
against the assented Act's own text; treat the underlying requirements as reliable since they
mirror standard Guyanese business-registration steps, but re-confirm the exact wording once the
Bank publishes its own application requirements]

A client preparing to apply once the Bank is operational needs, in this order:

1. **Business registration** — legally registered with the Deeds and Commercial Registries
   (DCRA).
2. **GRA TIN** — a Taxpayer Identification Number from GRA.
3. **NIS registration** — registered with the National Insurance Scheme, covering contributions
   for the owner and any employees.
4. **The right local licences** — whatever licence(s) or permit(s) the specific business type
   requires (trade licence, food handler's certificate, sector-specific permits, etc.).
5. **A business plan answering four questions directly** — what the business is, who the
   customers are, how it makes money, and what the loan is specifically for.

**Every business plan this Desk produces should already satisfy all five** — items 1–4 are
standard advisory-desk practice regardless of funding source, and item 5 is the plan's own core
content. Treat a gap in any of these five as a live Deficiency Listing item, not just a GDB-
specific one — see `plan-intake-desk`'s Guardrails for how this is enforced.

## 3. Commercial bank SME lending

### Bank of Guyana published rates [V] — Annual Report 2025

| Rate (% p.a.) | 2023 | 2024 | **2025** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small Savings Rate | 0.81 | 0.81 | **0.81** |
| Weighted Avg. Lending Rate | 8.36 | 8.07 | **7.90** |
| Prime Lending Rate | 8.38 | 8.38 | **8.20** |

Policy rate held at 5.0%. **⚠ The Bank of Guyana does not publish a distinct small business lending
rate.** Treat any claim of an official Guyanese SME rate series as unconfirmed.

**The core economics: deposits cost banks under 1%; SMEs pay 9–14%.**

### Liquidity is not the constraint — collateral is

**[V]** Private sector credit rose 18.0% to **G$531,790m** at end-2025. **Real estate mortgages are
35% of private credit; agriculture just 6%.** Bank loans are only 47.5% of domestic investments while
securities holdings grew 32.9%. **Excess liquid assets ratio 96.3%.**

**Banks have ample funds and are parking them in Treasury bills. The binding constraint on SME
credit is collateral and information, not liquidity.** This matters for advice: a client declined
for a loan usually has a security or records problem, not a bad business.

### Published SME terms by bank

| Bank | Published rate | Size range | Max tenor | Equity/margin | Rate published? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Demerara Bank** | **11–13%** | G$5m–G$25m | **12 yrs** | **20% equity** | Yes |
| **Citizens Bank** | **9–14%** | not stated | 10 yrs | not stated | Yes |
| **Bank of Baroda** | **10–12% (neg.)** | not stated | 10 yrs | **25–40% margin** | Yes |
| **Republic Bank** | not published | <G$25m (SME tier) | not stated | not stated | No |
| **GBTI** | not published | not stated | 1–3 yrs (GROW) | secured **or unsecured** | No |
| **Scotiabank Guyana** | no data | no data | no data | no data | No |

**The 9–14% band is reliable for SME term credit.**

**Republic Bank [V]** — segments by facility size: SME below G$25m · Commercial G$25m–G$70m ·
Corporate above G$70m. Application fee **G$4,500** (loans to G$5m) or **G$7,500** (over G$5m).
Corporate front-end fee ½% of amount requested. **⚠ Note the documentation cliff: SMEs need only an
income statement, but Commercial tier requires 3 years of financials plus valuations. Crossing
G$25m materially raises the paperwork burden** — worth planning a facility around.

**GBTI [U — site blocked all fetches; confirm directly]** — **GBTI GROW** SME programme launched
Nov 2024. Four focus areas: retail/wholesale trading and distribution; agriculture; manufacturing;
commercial vehicles. **Both secured and unsecured loans — the most SME-friendly collateral stance in
the market.** Tenors: 1 yr stock loans, 3 yrs business outfitting, 2 yrs agriculture. **GBTI
describes itself as the largest lender to Guyana's agricultural sector** — preferential rates,
production-cycle repayment, **1–6 month moratorium on instalments. If the client is agri, GBTI is
the first call.**

**Demerara Bank [V] — the most transparent lender.** Small Scale G$5m–G$10m; Medium G$10m–G$25m.
**11–13% reducing balance. Maximum 12 years — longest published tenor. 20% equity contribution
required (80% LTC ceiling).** Security: first mortgage, first debenture, cash, bill of sale.

**Citizens Bank [V]** — **9–14%**, 1–10 years, property-secured by default with bank-approved
valuation. **Most demanding on corporate governance paperwork** — all directors' IDs, 2 years annual
returns, 3 years audited financials, business plan, 1+ year bank statements.

**Bank of Baroda [V]** — **10–12% negotiable**, max 10 years, **loan margin 25–40% (LTV ~60–75%) —
most conservative gearing in the market.** The only bank explicitly requiring **forward-looking
3-year projections and insurance** as standard. Single branch, Georgetown.

**Scotiabank Guyana [V as to status]** — remains in Guyana; from 1 Nov 2025 operations transferred
to a locally incorporated subsidiary. Posture reads corporate/transaction-banking led. **No SME
products, rates or terms retrievable. Do not assume an SME offering exists.**

### The universal loan application package

Assembling these once covers essentially every bank:

1. Business registration certificate (or Certificate of Incorporation + Memorandum/Articles)
2. TIN — business and **every signatory/director**
3. Valid photo ID for all owners/directors
4. Proof of address dated within 6 months
5. **Financial statements, last 3 years** (audited if incorporated)
6. **Cashflow projections** (Baroda wants 3 years forward)
7. **Bank statements, 12 months minimum**
8. **Business plan** stating purpose of financing
9. Quotation / price list / agreement of sale / letter of intent for the asset financed
10. **Certificate of valuation by a bank-approved valuator**, transport/title, photos of collateral
11. GRA Certificate of Compliance and NIS compliance
12. **CreditInfo credit report**

**Three routes for a collateral-short SME:** GBTI GROW (only bank advertising unsecured SME
lending — confirm) · SBB guarantee (40%/70%, up to G$30m, via Republic or GBTI — underused) ·
Guyana Development Bank (Act assented 30 Jul 2026, not yet operational — see §2).

**⚠ No bank has an SME lending product explicitly tied to oil, gas or local content.** The financing
constraint on local content is officially recognised but banks have launched nothing against it.
Do not imply such facilities exist.

## 4. IPED — effectively the SME lending market

**[V]** Incorporated 2 Oct 1985, operational 1 April 1986. Company limited by guarantee,
tax-exempt Organisation of National Character, all surpluses reinvested. Guyana's oldest and largest
MFI. 253–254 South Road, Bourda, Georgetown · +592-226-4675 · iped@ipedgy.com

### Loan sizes [V, with one conflict]

Advertised range **G$40,000 – G$35,000,000**, but the product page caps most products at G$7.5m:

| Product | Max (GYD) | Physical collateral? |
|---|---|---|
| Poultry / Crop / Livestock / Vending / Fishing / Logging / Transportation | 7,500,000 each | Yes (if ≥G$2m) |
| **Mining** | 7,500,000 | **None** |
| **Micro Vending** | 2,000,000 | **None** |
| **Market Vending** | 500,000 | **None** |

**Treat G$7.5m as the verified working maximum for standard sector products; G$35m as an advertised
institutional ceiling requiring confirmation.**

Four windows: **Youth Loan Facility (18–35)**, Micro, Small, Medium. Plus Micro Boost and a Green
Loan Facility under the IDB EcoMicro pilot.

### Rates [M — verify directly]

Reported **36% down to 12% p.a. reducing balance** depending on loan size; lower with cash
collateral. Plus **processing fee 1% of loan (min G$4,000)** and revenue stamp 0.1%.

**⚠ These come from IPED's own cost-of-borrowing PDFs, which are image files that would not parse —
the figures are search-indexed, not directly read, and the files are dated December 2022.**
Independent corroboration: IPED's 2021 accounts imply a portfolio yield of ~19–20%, consistent with
a 12–36% posted range weighted toward larger, cheaper loans.

### Collateral — the commercially decisive fact

- Loans **≥ G$2m**: mortgage on property and/or bill of sale on vehicles and equipment
- Loans **< G$2m**: bill of sale on vehicles/equipment
- **No physical collateral:** Market Vending, Micro Vending, Mining

**In 2021, 72% of principal borrowers had no real estate collateral** — 899 real-estate-secured
loans vs 3,161 not. **IPED is a genuine substitute for collateral-based bank lending, not a mirror
of it.** For a client with a viable business and no transported title, IPED is usually the answer.

### Reach and scale

**14 branches [V]:** Regions 1 (Mabaruma, Port Kaituma), 2 (Anna Regina, Charity), 3 (Parika,
Vreed-en-Hoop), 4 (Georgetown HQ, Lusignan), 5 (Rosignol/Bushlot), 6 (Port Mourant, New Amsterdam,
Corriverton), 9 (Lethem), 10 (Linden). **⚠ IPED claims all ten regions but Regions 7 and 8 have no
listed branch** — probably served by mobile officers.

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Cumulative loans (40 yrs) | ~147,000–150,000 |
| Cumulative value | **G$64 billion+** |
| Active clients | ~5,000 |
| Women borrowers | ~40% |
| Borrowers under 25 | **~5%** |
| **2025 approvals** | **G$5.7 billion** (2020: G$2.9bn) |

**Portfolio character (FY2021):** rural **94%** of loans by number · agriculture (rice, crops,
poultry, livestock, fishing) **49.4%** · retail trade 28.8% · **small manufacturing only 3.0%** —
a notable gap. Women were 34% of loans by number but only **20% by value** — women borrow materially
smaller amounts. NPLs 8.0%, down from 10.8%.

**⚠ Do not cite the "average loan size 162" figure from IPED's AR2021 p.16 — it is a data error in
the source. The correct implied 2021 average is approximately G$794,000.**

**Business Development Unit [V]** — training in management, accounting, marketing and technical
services, with an explicit remit to transform clients **"from informal to formal"**: facilitating
registration, tax compliance, insurance, networking. Staffed with a Senior Business Counsellor.
**Strategically important, since the Guyana Development Bank will require registration + TIN.**

**2026 IDB Invest / JICA facility [V]** — **US$5,000,000** (US$2.5m IDB Invest + US$2.5m JICA
TADAC), 5 years with up to 1-year grace, on-lent to MSMEs with focus on **women-led**, plus youth
and rural. Signed 19 Dec 2025, in implementation.

## 5. Other microfinance and credit unions

**SBDF (Small Business Development Finance Trust)** — the second MFI. **The public record stops in
2018.** Most recent confirmed lending: **G$279m (2017)**, essentially flat in nominal terms from
G$274m in 2010 — a real contraction. **Roughly one-twentieth of IPED's scale.** Current status,
products and rates could not be confirmed.

**⚠ There is no institution called "Guyana Microfinance Institution."** The two real MFIs are
**IPED and SBDF.**

### Credit unions — not an SME channel

**[U — CCCU statistics carry no date stamp and are internally inconsistent]** 19 credit unions ·
38,737 members · loans **US$31.4m** · assets US$58.6m.

**Membership is ~1% of Guyana's population.** The stated cause is that most Guyanese credit unions
are **"closed bond"** — restricted to employees of a particular employer or sector.

**The decisive finding: total credit union lending across the entire movement (~G$6.6bn) is only
marginally larger than IPED's single-year 2025 approvals of G$5.7bn — and because the bond is
closed and employment-based, credit unions are overwhelmingly consumer lenders to salaried
employees, not small business lenders. Do not model them as an SME channel.**

**GPSCCU** (the largest) — Character Loan up to 75% of savings balance, unsecured · Secured Loan max
G$5m · Mortgage max G$15m · first loan capped at **G$300,000** · eligibility after 6 monthly
contributions. **⚠ GPSCCU states "1% on the reducing balance." A 1% annual rate is implausible —
this is almost certainly 1% per month ≈ 12% p.a. Do not cite "1%" as an annual rate.**
**GPSCCU publishes no commercial or business loan product.**

### Women and youth

**WIIN (Women Innovation and Investment Network) [V, active]** — Ministry of Human Services.
Training → business plan → evaluation → **grant** (not a loan). Funded by Global Affairs Canada and
the IDB. Dec 2025: 83 women, **G$300,000 each**. Mar 2026: 54 women, G$300,000 each.
**Target population is specific:** survivors of trafficking, survivors of violence, and women
seeking economic betterment. **As much a gender-based-violence recovery instrument as an enterprise
programme.** Scale: ~G$41m total — meaningful socially, negligible next to IPED's G$5.7bn/year.

**Youth finance is essentially unserved.** ~5% of IPED borrowers under 25, unchanged across five
years. No dedicated youth lender, no youth-specific rate or collateral concession.

**Board of Industrial Training (BIT)** — **correction: BIT is a skills-training agency and does not
operate microcredit.** Free 4–6 month trade courses with a monthly stipend. Upstream of finance.

**⚠ HEYS, POWER, and Small Grants for Women-run Microenterprises reference ministry configurations
that no longer exist. Likely legacy or discontinued — do not represent as live.**

### Remittances

**[V]** 2023 remittances **~US$550m, +44% vs 2019** — roughly equal to the entire education budget.
**⚠ No remittance-backed or remittance-history-based lending product exists in Guyana** — a
well-established model elsewhere in the Caribbean. **This appears to be a genuine market gap.**

## 6. GO-Invest and investment incentives

**[V]** 190 Camp & Church Streets, Georgetown · +592-225-0658 · invest@guyanainvest.gov.gy
Two sites exist; **guyanainvest.gov.gy is the active one — goinvest.gov.gy repeatedly failed to load.**

### The single most useful distinction: automatic vs discretionary

**AUTOMATIC — claim as of right if you meet the criteria:**
- Export allowance (statutory formula)
- Unlimited loss carry-forward
- **Standing VAT zero-rating and duty relief on inputs for licensed manufacturers**
- Repatriation guarantees, non-discrimination, expropriation protection
- **Budget 2026 sectoral removals — agriculture/agro-processing corporate tax; VAT on local
  furniture and jewellery**
- SBB 20% government procurement set-aside

**DISCRETIONARY — case-by-case, ministerial:**
- **Tax holidays** — the State Department notes they are **"less likely to be granted than duty-free
  status or a VAT waiver."** Do not plan around one
- Investment Agreements for duty/excise/VAT waivers
- Accelerated depreciation — "pending approval on a case specific basis"
- Access to land, factory space and licences

**Advise clients to model the base case on automatic incentives only, and treat any negotiated
holiday or waiver as upside.** Documented weaknesses in the discretionary layer: de facto screening,
ministers retaining significant authority, broad GRA discretion to interpret tax law, and a
significant number of issues resolved in cabinet without communicated timelines.

### Tax holidays [V as to mechanism]

Exemption from corporation tax on income from qualifying **new economic activity**: up to **5 years**
standard, **10 years** for listed high-impact activities, beyond 10 for renewable-energy
infrastructure. Two gateways — geographic (hinterland regions; **[U]** whether 1/8/9/10 or 1/7/8/9/10)
and sectoral.

Qualifying [V]: non-traditional agriculture and **agro-processing** (*excluding sugar refining, rice
milling, chicken farming*) · **ICT** (excluding retail/distribution) · petroleum and minerals ·
**tourist hotels** · **value-added wood processing** · **textiles** · bio-technology · new
pharmaceuticals · infrastructure including renewable energy and cold storage.

**Trade-off: while on a tax holiday, no wear-and-tear allowances may be claimed.** Losses incurred
during the holiday may be set off without limitation against income immediately following expiry.

### Duty / excise / VAT waivers — the workhorse

**The decisive criterion is *value addition*.** Per the State Department: "A key factor in the
determination of duty-free status and value added tax waiver is value addition." **This is the most
useful practical insight for advising a client on incentives.**

**The Investment Agreement process [V]:** investor approaches GO-Invest → GO-Invest prepares the
agreement plus an **itemised list of goods** and submits to GRA → GRA reviews, may require a meeting
and **site visit** → **Minister of Finance approves and signs**, retaining final discretion → GRA
implements → **the investor must then apply to GRA case-by-case for each item, receiving a CG letter
per request.**

**⚠ The most important operational point: an Investment Agreement is not a blanket waiver. It is a
pre-approved shopping list against which each individual importation must be separately cleared.
Budget for that administrative overhead.**

### Do SMEs qualify?

**[V] There is no published general minimum investment amount.** But because incentives are granted
by ministerial discretion, **scale, employment creation and value addition function as de facto
screens.** A small operator can apply; whether they get an agreement is another matter.

Sector thresholds that do exist: **tourism** — minimum 30 rooms coastal or 8 rooms interior for
duty/VAT exemption on capital items; **export allowance** — minimum 10% of sales exported.

**The realistic SME path is not a negotiated tax holiday.** It is SBB registration → procurement
set-aside and concessionary finance; the GDB once live; and standing manufacturer duty/VAT
zero-rating on inputs.

### Export allowance

Deduction from chargeable profits for exports of manufactured, processed or agricultural products
**outside CARICOM**. **25% to 75%**, scaled to export/total sales ratio; **minimum 10% of sales must
be exported.** Excluded "traditional" products: rice, sugar, bauxite, gold, diamonds, timber, lumber,
petroleum, shrimp, molasses, rum. **Budget 2026 added timber value-added products.**

**⚠ Note the tension: CARICOM is the easiest market (duty-free), but the export allowance only
applies outside CARICOM.** Market access and tax optimisation point in different directions.

### Industrial estates

**Coldingen ~95% occupancy · Eccles ~95% occupancy · Lethem under construction · Belvedere in
development.** **⚠ At ~95% occupancy, space is effectively unavailable at Eccles and Coldingen** —
a material constraint for a client seeking factory space.

**Special Development Zones — announced, NOT legislated.** Budget 2026 announced designated zones
(Lethem, Linden, Enmore, Wales). **[V negative] As of 18 Jul 2026 no SDZ legislation has been tabled
or passed.** Treat as policy intent without an operative legal framework.

### Budget 2026 — key changes [V], passed 14 Feb 2026

| Measure | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Corporate tax removed on agriculture & agro-processing** | **The largest single change — converts a discretionary tax holiday into an automatic sectoral zero rate** |
| Export allowance expanded | Timber value-added products added |
| VAT removed | Locally manufactured furniture and jewellery |
| Duty & VAT removed | Security cameras and alarm systems |
| Childcare & elderly care | Corporate tax removed; G$1.5bn co-investment |
| Guyana Development Bank | US$100m injection |
| Income tax threshold | → **G$140,000/month** |

**CDB [V]** — total approvals to Guyana 1982–2026: **US$717m**. **⚠ No current CDB MSME line of
credit through Guyanese financial intermediaries was found** — treat as NOT FOUND rather than
confirmed absent.

across all ten regions. **CRAF (Credit Risk Abatement Facility)** supports SMEs and financial
institutions to finance renewable energy and energy efficiency projects — **arguably more relevant

## 8. Local Content Act 2021 — the highest-leverage action for many SMEs

**Primary source [V]:** Official Gazette (Extraordinary) No. 297/2021, read page-by-page.
**As of 18 July 2026 no amendment has been passed.** Register: `lcregister.petroleum.gov.gy`

### Definition of a "Guyanese company" — s.2 [V]

- Beneficially owned by Guyanese nationals holding voting rights of **at least 51% of total issued
  shares**; **AND**
- Guyanese nationals holding **at least 75% of executive and senior management positions** and
  **at least 90% of non-managerial positions**

**Limbs are joined by "and" — both must be satisfied. The staffing thresholds are not optional.**
"Guyanese national" is defined simply as a citizen of Guyana.

### s.6(8) — why the certificate matters

> "A Guyanese national or Guyanese company who is not registered on the Local Content Register shall
> not be measured for the purpose of compliance with the minimum local content levels."

**If you are not on the Register, buying from you does not count toward an operator's quota — so
operators will not buy from you. The certificate is the market-access key.**

**Certification is annual, renewable on the anniversary of issuance [V, s.6(3)].**
**Reportedly free [U — not confirmed on an official page; verify].**

**Processing timelines from Jan/Feb 2026 [V]:**

| Applicant | New | Renewal |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorships / landlords | **5 working days** | 3 days |
| 100% Guyanese-owned companies & partnerships | 15 days | 10 days |
| Other firms (JVs meeting the 51% test) | 21 days | 15 days |

**~1,300–1,500 certified entities as of mid-2026** (1,315 confirmed Sep 2025). Over **US$2 billion**
procured from Guyanese companies since enactment; **US$743m in 2024** alone.

### First Schedule — the 40 ring-fenced service areas [V]

**Tier 1 — highest legal protection, lowest technical barrier. Best SME entry points:**

| Category | Floor |
|---|---|
| Immigration support / work permits, visas | **100%** |
| Customs brokerage | **100%** |
| Ground transportation – personnel | **100%** |
| Local insurance | **100%** |
| Security services | **95%** |
| Pest control | **95%** |
| Janitorial and laundry | **90%** |
| Catering | **90%** |
| Office space / accommodation rental | **90%** |
| Lay down yard facility | **90%** |
| Accounting / legal services | **90%** |

**Tier 2 — solid floors, moderate barriers:** Trucking 75% · Food supply 75% · Surveying 75% ·
Cargo management 75% · Industrial cleaning onshore 75% · Administrative support & facilities
management 75% · **Non-hazardous waste 75%** · Marketing/advertising 75% · Ventilation 70% ·
Warehousing 60% · Construction work for buildings onshore 50% · Equipment rental 50% ·
Manpower and crewing 50% · Export packaging/crating 50%.

**Tier 3 — large money, weak legal protection:** Structural fabrication onshore 30% · Pipe sand
blasting/coating 30% · Pipe welding onshore 25% · Ship & rig chandlery 25% · Environmental services
25% · **Hazardous waste 25%** · Medical 25% · ICT network 20% · Borehole testing 20% · Aviation
support 20% · Dredging 10% · Metrology 10% · **Engineering and machining 5%**.

**The paradox worth naming to clients: Engineering and machining has the lowest floor (5%) but the
second-largest cumulative spend (US$250m); manpower and crewing has a 50% floor and the largest
(US$387m). Volume and legal protection are inversely correlated.** An SME chasing revenue looks at
Tier 3; an SME chasing defensible market share starts in Tier 1.

*Waste management is a two-speed market — non-hazardous 75% vs hazardous 25%. The hazardous tier
requires permits and specialist handling and is a credible upgrade path.*

### Two provisions to quote in tenders [V]

- **s.13(3)** — contracts must be awarded via competitive bidding initiated by a widely circulated
  public tender; sole sourcing only with Ministerial approval
- **s.13(5)** — where bids are equal or **within five percent of each other** when financially
  evaluated, **the bid containing the highest level of local content shall be selected**
- **s.13(6)** — contractors must hold **half-yearly workshops** on procurement process, qualification
  and bid evaluation criteria. **These are a legal entitlement, not a courtesy — advise clients to
  attend**

The Procurement Sub-Plan obliges contractors to offer Guyanese firms **"contract and payment terms
that facilitate competitive financing and growth"** — the statutory basis for pushing back on
90–120 day payment terms.

### Penalties [V]

Operating without meeting minimum local content: **G$50m** — the largest in the Act. False
information to obtain a certificate: G$10m. **A Guyanese company aiding or abetting a contravention
(fronting): G$5m individual / G$10m body corporate, plus being struck off the Register.**
**s.24: where a body corporate offends with the consent, connivance or neglect of a director or
manager, that individual is personally guilty and liable to the same penalty.**

**⚠ Warn clients considering a "rent-a-citizen" arrangement: the Guyanese party commits an offence
in its own right.** Government stated in June 2026 that it has closed this loophole through
screening of annual plans.

**⚠ s.7(2) lets the Minister amend the First Schedule by Order under negative resolution — the
percentages can change without an amending Act and with little warning.** Budget 2026 committed to
revising the Schedule to expand coverage. Assume floors can move upward.

### Barriers SMEs actually hit

- **Financing.** One SME could secure only invoice-based lending at **80% of invoice value** —
  insufficient to cover rent, phones, PPE, transport
- **Exploitative JV structures.** Capital injected as a **loan** to the JV rather than equity;
  equipment leased at inflated rates — stripping economics out before profit-sharing. One Trinidadian
  partner offered a Guyanese counterpart only **10% ownership**
- **The conversion gap.** The Centre for Local Business Development trained 3,000+ firms; **fewer
  than 1,500 secured contracts. Registration ≠ revenue**
- **No "de-bunching" clause** obliging contractors to break large bundled packages into
  SME-accessible lots; **s.7(3)(b) capacity waivers can persist 10+ years**

**Prequalification [U as to Guyana specifics]** — the general industry pattern is ISO 9001, ISO
45001, sometimes ISO 14001 or ISO 29001, plus **HSE-MS documentation (3-year TRIR/DART data, OSHA
300A summaries, bridging documents)** and Approved Vendor List placement. **JIB is a UK scheme and
is almost certainly not applicable in Guyana — do not assert it.**

### Centre for Local Business Development [V]

253–254 South Road, Bourda, 3rd Floor, Georgetown · +592-223-7781 · centreguyana.com
Established by the Stabroek co-venturers; a local not-for-profit. Over **7,000 registered
businesses** on its hub. Assisted 100 companies in building **HSSE Management Systems**.
Programmes: **AccelerateHer 2026** (women), **ElevateAll** (ICT), Wood-Based Accelerator.

**The HSSE Management System mentorship is the single most directly useful offering for an SME
trying to clear the prequalification gate** — operators audit HSE-MS documentation, not ISO paper
alone.

**Bottom line: the Local Content Certificate — free or near-free, issued in 5–21 days, renewable
annually — is a disproportionately cheap option on the entire ring-fenced market. It should be step
one for any Guyanese SME in a listed category, regardless of whether they currently serve the
sector.**

**Use non-oil growth, not headline growth, in any business plan.** Headline GDP is oil; non-oil
growth of 8–12% is what an SME's market actually experiences.

**The FX premium is the most immediately actionable macro fact.** A September 2025 nine-point FX
control plan requires importers to present invoice, bill of lading and GRA tax compliance to banks
before USD is released. **Any plan costing imports at G$208.50 understates them. Model at cambio
rates and build in queue risk.**

**Domestic market reality:** ~1 million people, comparable to a mid-sized city, and most of the
US$34bn nominal GDP is oil accruing to the state and the consortium — **not addressable household
consumption.** CARICOM (15 members, ~US$90bn combined GDP) is the offset, and Guyana is positioned
as the region's intended breadbasket under CARICOM's food-import-reduction initiative (originally
"25 by 2025," **missed and extended to 2030**).

**Sector growth 2026 targets:** Sugar +67.9% *(base effect off a collapsed 2024, not a healthy
industry)* · **Construction +25.4%** · Bauxite +19.3% · Oil & gas +17.9% · Agriculture +7.6% ·
Services +6.6% · **Rice +1.8%** (slowing sharply from +12.4%).

**Where SME demand concentrates:** construction · manufacturing · logistics and shorebase services ·
accommodation, catering and facilities management · agriculture and food supply ·
power-cost-sensitive manufacturing *if* Gas-to-Energy delivers.

**Key risks to flag in any plan:** FX shortage and parallel premium · fiscal dependence on oil
drawdowns (the 2026 NRF withdrawal already *fell* on softer prices) · **electricity delivery
slippage — Gas-to-Energy has missed dates repeatedly; do not model cheap power before 2027 at the
earliest** · skills shortage and wage inflation · Dutch-disease pressure on non-oil tradables ·
the Venezuela border controversy over Essequibo, flagged by the World Bank as an elevated risk.
