## 1. Business registration

### The formalisation sequence [V]

Advise the next rung, not the top of the ladder:

| Step | Body | Cost | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Business name registration | **DCRA** | **G$6,500** first registration; **G$2,500** annual renewal | up to **3 business days** |
| 2. TIN | **GRA** | application free; **G$1,000** for the certificate | — |
| 3. VAT registration *(if over threshold)* | GRA | — | — |
| 4. NIS employer registration | **NIS** | — | — |
| 5. Local council business/trade licence | M&CC or RDC | [U] | — |
| 6. Sector-specific licences | various | see §6 | — |

**Company incorporation** under the Companies Act 1991 at the Commercial Registry takes **5–10
business days**; express processing (50–100% premium) reduces this to 2–5 days **[U]**.

**[V] Registration timing has genuinely improved** — from three weeks to under one week through
digitisation.

**Total headline cost is ~G$7,500 (~US$36) for business name plus TIN. Registration cost is not the
barrier to formalisation.** The barrier is the ongoing compliance burden and the competitive
advantage of remaining invisible — roughly half the economy operates informally (see
`guyana-operating-context.md` §6.2). Acknowledge that honestly and lead with what formalisation
*unlocks*: credit, contracts, government procurement, Local Content certification.

### Choosing a legal form

| Form | When it fits | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Sole trader** (business name registration) | Starting out, one owner, low liability exposure | No limited liability — personal assets are exposed. Banks discount it |
| **Partnership** | Two or more owners | **⚠ Under the Termination Act s.21(4)(c), employment ceasing on dissolution of a partnership can extinguish severance entitlement in defined circumstances** — get this drafted properly |
| **Limited liability company** (Companies Act 1991) | Liability exposure, bank credibility, contracting eligibility, Local Content 51% test | Annual returns, audited financials for some lenders, higher compliance load |

**Owners commonly stay sole traders past the point where limited liability, bank credibility, or
contracting eligibility justifies incorporating.** Flag this when a client is bidding for
institutional work, taking on staff, or approaching a bank for anything material.

**⚠ On business sale or transfer** — under the Termination Act **s.4(5)**, prior service carries over
to a successor employer **only if the successor has agreed** [V]. This is a real trap in Guyanese
asset sales. Raise it before, not after.

## 2. Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA)

### Corporate income tax

| Category | Rate | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial companies | **40%** | [V] |
| Non-commercial companies | **25%** | [V] |
| **Agriculture and agro-processing** | **Fully exempt from 2026** (Budget 2026) | **[V]** |
| Telephone companies | 45% | [U] |
| Minimum turnover tax | 2% of turnover | [U] — Budget 2026 changed sectoral rates; verify |

**[V] The Budget 2026 removal of corporate tax on agriculture and agro-processing is the single
largest fiscal incentive available to a Guyanese small business today.** It converts what used to be
a discretionary, ministerially-granted tax holiday into an **automatic sectoral zero rate** — no
application, no Investment Agreement, no negotiation. Raise it with every agri client.

### PAYE — 2026 [V]

| Item | 2026 |
|---|---|
| **Threshold** | **G$140,000/month** (raised from G$130,000) |
| Rate to G$3.36m/yr chargeable | **25%** |
| Rate above G$3.36m/yr chargeable | **35%** |
| Child deduction | G$10,000/month per child under 18 |

**[Inference worth stating to clients]** Below G$140,000/month there is no PAYE. **This is why many
SMEs cluster wages at or just under G$140,000 — it is the point where the employee's take-home is
maximised per dollar of employer cost.** Combined with the 8.4% employer NIS, a G$140,000 salary
costs the employer ~G$151,760 and the employee keeps the full G$140,000 less their 5.6% NIS.

### VAT [V]

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| **Registration threshold** | **G$15,000,000** in annual taxable supplies |
| **Standard rate** | **14%** |
| Import basis | CIF **+ duty + all other taxes** — VAT compounds on the duty-inclusive value |

**Two things to warn clients about:**

1. **The threshold is a cliff, not a slope.** A business approaching G$15m needs to know it is coming
   *before* it crosses, because prices must effectively rise or margin absorbs the 14%. Model this
   in advance.
2. **Below the threshold you cannot reclaim input VAT on imports.** For a growing importer sitting
   just under the line, voluntary registration may be worth modelling — the input credit can
   outweigh the compliance cost.

**[V] Budget 2026 VAT removals** effective 16 Feb 2026: vehicles under 1500cc (under 4 years) and
hybrids under 2000cc · **all import duties and taxes on ATVs and outboard engines up to 150hp** ·
**fertilisers, agrochemicals, pesticides, and farming/mining machinery** · locally manufactured
furniture and jewellery · security cameras and alarm systems.
**[V] Renewable energy technologies are VAT-exempt on import** — this materially improves
solar-plus-battery economics against a diesel generator.

### Import duties and levies [V]

| Charge | Rate |
|---|---|
| Common External Tariff | **5–20%** typical; up to **40%** agricultural |
| Environmental levy | **G$10/unit** per non-returnable beverage container, imported **and** domestic |
| Excise — alcohol / tobacco / fuel | 40% / 100% / 50% |

**The tax stack compounds:** a 20% CET item lands at roughly **CIF × 1.20 × 1.14 ≈ 1.37× CIF** before
broker, port and haulage costs.

### Filing, compliance certificates, and arrears

**Unfiled returns are the single most common blocker to funding.** Check this early in every
engagement — before doing plan or funding work, not after. A GRA **Certificate of Compliance** is
required for:
- Small Business Bureau registration and grants
- SBB-guaranteed loans
- The Guyana Development Bank (when operational)
- **Foreign exchange release by commercial banks** for imports (since the Sept 2025 FX controls)
- Work permit applications

**⚠ Needs verification:** exact filing deadlines, late-filing and late-payment penalty rates,
withholding tax rates and scope, property tax, capital gains tax, and whether payment arrangements
for arrears are available and on what terms. **Do not answer these from memory — direct the client
to GRA or a tax practitioner.**

**Tone on arrears.** Clients are often frightened or embarrassed about unfiled returns, which is
exactly why they went unaddressed. Be matter-of-fact: these are common, they are fixable, and the
authorities generally prefer a business that comes forward. Do not moralise — and do not soften the
actual exposure either.

## 3. National Insurance Scheme (NIS)

| Item | Rate | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Employee contribution** | **5.6%** of gross | [V] |
| **Employer contribution** | **8.4%** of gross | [V] |
| **Insurable earnings ceiling** | **G$280,000/month** | [V] |
| Self-employed | contributes separately | [U] — rate needs verification |

**Practical employer cost:** on a G$100,000 wage, employer NIS adds G$8,400 → true cost **~G$108,400
(~US$519)/month**. Always include the employer contribution in any staffing cost model — owners
routinely budget the salary and forget the 8.4%.

**Who must be registered:** all employees — **including part-time, casual, and family members who
are actually employed**. This is the most commonly missed obligation in small Guyanese businesses.

**Say this plainly to owners:** unremitted NIS deductions are money held on the employee's behalf.
Both the arrears and the exposure survive. It is not a cash-flow buffer.

**NIS compliance is a gate**, not just an obligation — required for SBB registration, SBB-guaranteed
loans, the Guyana Development Bank, and work permit applications.

**⚠ Needs verification:** self-employed contribution rate and bands, registration forms and process,
and penalties for non-remittance.

## 4. Labour law

**Governing statute: Termination of Employment and Severance Pay Act, Cap. 99:08** (also cited as
Cap. 96:01 — both citations circulate; use "Cap. 99:08 (also cited as Cap. 96:01)" to be safe).
Commenced 7 August 1997. **Amended once only, by Act No. 7 of 1999. No amendment since 1999** [V].

Everything in this section was read directly from two independent primary sources — the L.R.O. 1/2012
consolidated text and Act No. 19 of 1997 as passed — and cross-checked section by section. **This is
the highest-confidence material in this file.**

**⚠ s.3 makes the Act a floor only.** Collective agreements may set higher standards, and any
agreement limiting the Act to the employee's detriment is **void** (s.3(3)).

### Minimum wage

**[V] Private sector: G$60,147/month** (G$2,776/day, G$347/hour) — National Minimum Wage Order No. 20
of 2022, effective 1 July 2022. **⚠ [U] Still G$60,147 as of late 2025; a December 2025 motion to
raise it to G$100,000 was tabled. Status as of July 2026 unconfirmed — verify before advising.**

**[V] Public sector floor: G$100,000/month.**

**The ~66% gap between the private legal minimum and the public sector floor is the most important
wage fact for a Guyanese SME.** A business paying the legal minimum is paying materially below what
the state pays for unskilled labour, which makes **G$100,000, not G$60,147, the effective reservation
wage.** Advising a client to pay the legal minimum is legally correct and commercially useless.

### Notice periods — s.15 [V]

| Length of service | Notice |
|---|---|
| **Less than one year** | **2 weeks** |
| **One year or more** | **1 month** |

**⚠ Correct a very common error: there are only two bands. Notice does NOT graduate with length of
service.** A 25-year employee is entitled to the same one month's statutory notice as a 13-month
employee.

Other operative points [V]:
- Notice must be **in writing**
- Notice applies to **redundancy terminations too** (s.15(1) expressly covers s.12)
- The employee resigning owes the **same** notice periods (s.15(2))
- Notice does not apply where summary dismissal is available (s.15(3))
- **Notice may not be served during authorised leave** (s.15(4))
- Parties may contract for **longer** notice; an employer may waive its right to receive notice
  from the employee (s.15(5))

### Payment in lieu of notice — s.16 [V]

*"A sum equal to the **remuneration and benefits** due to the employee up to the expiry of any
required period of notice."*

**⚠ Note the different bases. Payment in lieu uses "remuneration and benefits" (s.16(1)). Severance
uses "basic wages excluding any allowance" (s.21(7)). These are two different calculations — do not
use one figure for both.**

s.16(2) is reciprocal — an employee leaving without notice owes the employer the equivalent sum.

### Severance / redundancy allowance — s.21 [V]

**Qualifying period: one or more completed years of continuous employment.**

| Service | Entitlement |
|---|---|
| Years 1–5 (including the entitlement year) | **1 week's wages per completed year** |
| After year 5, up to year 10 | **2 weeks' wages per completed year** |
| In excess of 10 years | **3 weeks' wages per completed year** |
| **Aggregate cap** | **52 weeks** |

**"Wages" means basic wages excluding any allowance** received at the time of termination (s.21(7)).
Allowances, overtime and bonuses are **excluded** from the severance base — commercially significant.

**No dollar cap exists.** Reaching the 52-week cap takes ~24 years' service.

**s.21(3): severance does not displace payment in lieu of notice — both are payable.**

**⚠ A widely-circulated wrong formula.** Several EOR and payroll vendor sites state "two weeks per
year for the first five years, four weeks per year thereafter." **That is wrong** and contradicted by
both primary texts. Do not rely on EOR vendor pages for this figure.

### Continuity of employment — s.4 [V]

Continuity runs from the first day of work and is **not** broken by: annual, maternity or sick leave ·
suspension · termination followed by reinstatement · temporary lay-off ≤6 weeks · lockout ·
contractual absence. **All those periods count toward length of service** (s.4(3)).

Exception: absence for lawful industrial action does not break continuity but **does not count**
toward length (s.4(4)).

**⚠ On business sale or transfer (s.4(5)): prior service carries over to the successor employer only
if the successor has agreed.** A real trap in asset sales — raise it during deal structuring.

**s.4(6): every employer must keep records sufficient to show compliance with the Act.** Failure =
fine of G$15,625 per offence.

### When severance is NOT payable — s.21(4) [V]

- **(a)** Summary dismissal under s.10; termination under s.9 (probation) or s.11 (misconduct,
  breach, or unsatisfactory performance)
- **(b)** The employee **unreasonably refuses**, in a s.12 redundancy, an offer of re-employment at
  the same place **or within a radius of ten miles**, on no less favourable conditions
- **(c)** Partnership dissolution, where the employee enters employment with one or more partners
  immediately after, or unreasonably refuses an offer on no less favourable terms
- **(d)** *[as amended 1999]* Retirement **at age sixty or such other agreed age**, where entitled to
  a gratuity or pension from the employer — **excluding NIS benefits**.
  **Proviso: where the gratuity is *less* than the s.21(1) severance, the severance amount is paid
  instead.** In effect, a retiring employee gets the **greater** of gratuity or statutory severance,
  and NIS benefits do not count against the entitlement

**Resignation** is implicitly excluded. **⚠ Genuine textual ambiguity:** the s.2 definition of
"severance of employment" says termination *"by an employee"* under s.7(a) or s.7(c)(ii), which on
its face could cover an employee who resigns on notice. This is almost certainly a drafting error for
"of an employee," but no judicial gloss was found. **[U] Flag it rather than assert either reading.**

**Penalties — s.21(5)–(6) [V]:** failure to pay severance = fine of **G$31,250 and imprisonment for
one year**; and on conviction the court **shall** additionally order the employer to pay the severance
sum.

### Redundancy — s.12 [V]

**Qualifying circumstances (s.12(2)):** modernisation/automation/mechanisation · discontinuance of
all or part of the business · sale or other disposition · **reorganisation to improve efficiency** ·
impossibility or impracticability due to shortage of materials, mechanical breakdown, *force majeure*
or act of God · **reduced operation made necessary by economic conditions**, including lack of or
change in markets, contraction in volume of work or sales, reduced demand, or surplus inventory.

**s.12(3) — the notification and consultation duty. Two points that are commonly got wrong:**

1. **The deadline is "as early as possible but not later than ONE MONTH from the date of the
   existence of any of the circumstances in subsection (2)."** The clock runs from when the
   **triggering circumstance arises**, not from the intended termination date.
2. **There is NO threshold by number of workers.** The duty applies to **any** redundancy
   termination, **including a single employee.**

**Who must be informed and consulted:** the recognised trade union, or if none exists, the employee
or the employee's representative — **and the Chief Labour Officer**.

**Must inform of:** the existence of the circumstances · the reasons for the contemplated termination
· **the number and categories of persons likely to be affected** · the period over which termination
is likely to be carried out · other relevant matters.

**Must consult on:** possible measures to **avert or mitigate** the adverse effects.

**⚠ The Act prescribes NO selection criteria** — no LIFO, no skills retention, no selection pools.
Selection is governed by any applicable collective agreement, and by s.8 (selecting on a s.8(1)
ground would be unfair). No statutory form is prescribed for the CLO notification.

**Penalty — s.12(4):** fine of **G$20,000 and imprisonment for three months** on summary conviction.

**Related [V]:** s.14 — lay-offs are unlawful except where the employer is empowered to terminate for
redundancy, and **no lay-off shall exceed six weeks**; penalty G$15,000. s.13 — on a business sale, if
the successor agrees to honour the transferor's obligations, they become binding on the successor.

### Dismissal for misconduct and performance — ss.9, 10, 11, 18 [V]

**Probation — s.9.** A new employee may be required to serve a probationary period, during which
**either party may terminate at any time for any reason and without notice**. "Probationary period"
is defined as **three months** from commencement, *"or such other period as may be agreed"* — so it
is extendable by agreement with no stated outer limit.

**Summary dismissal — s.10.** An employer may dismiss summarily **without notice or any severance or
terminal benefit** an employee **guilty of serious misconduct**. "Serious misconduct" means
misconduct such that the employer cannot reasonably be expected to take any course other than
termination (s.2). **s.10(2) narrows it considerably: the misconduct must be directly related to the
employment relationship and have a detrimental effect on the employer's business.** Off-duty conduct
with no business impact will not qualify. **The Act gives no enumerated list — it is a standard, not
a list.**

**Lesser misconduct and poor performance — s.11. This is the load-bearing compliance provision:**
- **(1)** For a breach of conditions, misconduct that is **not serious**, or misconduct the employer
  cannot be expected to tolerate if repeated — the employer **may give a written warning**
- **(2)** If the employee commits the same or similar offence **within the following six months**,
  the employer may terminate **without notice**. Note the six-month spent-warning window
- **(3) Waiver:** the employer is **deemed to have waived** the right to terminate for misconduct if
  it fails to act after having knowledge of the misconduct, or at the end of any investigation.
  **Delay forfeits the right to dismiss**
- **(4) Unsatisfactory performance:** employment **shall not** be terminated for unsatisfactory
  performance unless the employer has given **instructions as to how the employee should perform**
  and a **written warning** to adhere to them, and the employee **continues to perform
  unsatisfactorily**

**Disciplinary action short of dismissal — s.18.** Permitted where reasonable. Defined in order of
severity: (a) written warning, (b) suspension without pay. **Fines and monetary penalties are
prohibited (s.18(3)).** Reasonableness is judged by the nature of the violation, the employee's
duties, damage incurred, and previous conduct (s.18(4)). A complaint that disciplinary action is
unreasonable goes to the **Chief Labour Officer** (s.18(5)); if found unreasonable, the action is
withdrawn, withheld pay refunded, and the employee **reinstated** (s.18(6)).

**⚠ There is NO right to a hearing in this statute.** All 25 sections were read — there is no
provision requiring a hearing, an opportunity to respond, or a right to representation before
dismissal. What the Act requires procedurally is the **written warning chain (s.11)** and the
**reasonableness standard (s.18)**. A hearing requirement, where it binds an employer, comes from a
collective agreement, the contract, or common-law natural justice — **not** from Cap. 99:08.

**Because s.8(2) makes a failure of s.11 or s.18 obligations automatically unfair, the written
warning trail is the single most important thing a small employer can get right.** Advise clients to
document warnings contemporaneously and in writing, every time.

### Unfair dismissal — ss.8, 19, 20 [V]

**Automatically unfair reasons — s.8(1).** The following do **not** constitute good or sufficient
cause for dismissal or discipline:

- (a) race, sex, religion, colour, ethnic origin, national extraction, social origin, political
  opinion, **family responsibility**, or **marital status**
- (b) age, subject to any law or collective agreement on retirement
- (c) **a female employee's pregnancy or any reason connected with her pregnancy**
- (d) absence due to sickness or injury **certified by a registered medical practitioner**
- (e) absence due to compulsory military service or other civic obligation
- (f) participation in lawful industrial action
- (g) refusal to do work normally done by an employee engaged in industrial action under (f)
- (h) **filing a complaint or participating in proceedings against an employer alleging violation of
  any rule or law** — whistleblower/retaliation protection

**s.8(2):** a dismissal or disciplinary action is unfair if based on any s.8(1) ground **or on the
failure of any of the employer's obligations under s.11 or s.18**.

**Forum — s.19: the High Court**, not a labour tribunal.

**Remedies — s.20.** Compensation is *"such amount as the Court considers **just and equitable** in
all the circumstances having regard to the loss sustained... and the extent if any to which the
employee caused or contributed."* The Court may also award **"any other just remedy"** in addition to
or in lieu of compensation (s.20(3)).

**No statutory cap and no formula — compensation is at large**, on a just-and-equitable / actual-loss
basis with a contributory-fault discount.

**⚠ Reinstatement is not expressly named as a remedy for unfair dismissal.** The only express
reinstatement provision (s.18(6)) applies to *unreasonable discipline short of dismissal* determined
by the Chief Labour Officer. Reinstatement for unfair dismissal would have to come through s.20(3)'s
open-ended "any other just remedy". **[U] No Guyanese case law was accessible to confirm whether
courts order it in practice.**

### Certificate of termination — s.17 [V]

On termination, an employer **if so requested by the employee** shall provide a **certificate of
termination**. That is the entire section — it is **request-triggered**, and the Act prescribes **no
content, no form, and no time limit**.

**⚠ There is NO statutory right to written reasons for dismissal.** Notice must be in writing
(s.15(1)) and warnings must be in writing (s.11), but a statement of reasons is not mandated.

### Forum, enforcement and time limits [V]

| Claim | Forum |
|---|---|
| **Unfair dismissal / unfair discipline** (s.8 breach) | **High Court** (s.19, s.20) |
| **Unreasonable discipline** short of dismissal | **Chief Labour Officer** (s.18(5)–(6)) |
| **Recovery of severance / offences under the Act** | **Magistrate's Court**, summary jurisdiction (s.23) |

**s.22: the Chief Labour Officer may institute prosecutions**, and Department of Labour officers may
appear as prosecutor. **An employee owed severance can go to the Ministry of Labour rather than fund
litigation** — worth knowing when advising an employer on exposure.

**Time limits.** The Act specifies none. Under the incorporated Summary Jurisdiction (Procedure) Act
Cap. 10:02 s.6, where no time is specially limited, a complaint must be made **within six months from
when the matter of the complaint arose**. **[M]** — that inference (six months applies to the s.23
severance-recovery route) is a reading of the two statutes together, not something stated by a
Guyanese court.

**⚠ [U] The limitation period for a s.19 High Court unfair dismissal claim could not be verified.**
It is not in Cap. 99:08, and Cap. 10:02 s.6 addresses summary conviction offences, not a High Court
civil claim. **This must be checked with counsel before advising on a stale dismissal claim.**

**Penalties are trivial in 2026 terms** — these are 1997/1999 nominal figures. **The real exposure is
the s.21(6) court order to pay the severance itself, plus an uncapped s.20 award.** Do not let a
client be reassured by the size of the fines.

### Leave and hours [V]

| Item | Entitlement |
|---|---|
| Standard hours | 8/day, 40/week over ≤5 days |
| Annual leave | **12 days/yr** (1–10 years' service); **24 days/yr** after 10 years |
| Maternity | **13 weeks**, +13 for complications; NIS-paid |
| Sick leave | certified absence is an automatically unfair ground for dismissal (s.8(1)(d)) |

Governed by the Leave with Pay Act Cap. 99:02. **⚠ Verify current entitlements and any accrual and
carry-over rules directly.**

### The advisory line on terminations

**Terminations are the highest-risk routine event a small employer faces.** The exposure is uncapped
(s.20), the forum is the High Court, and the procedural requirements are easy to fail. **Always advise
professional input before any dismissal.** What this file can do is prepare the client to have an
informed conversation with an attorney — specifically: is there a written warning trail, was the
six-month window observed, was the right to dismiss waived by delay, and does the reason touch any
s.8(1) ground.

## 6. Licensing and sector permits

### ⚠ Correct this misconception first

**The trade licence is NOT a municipal licence.** It is a **national tax instrument issued by the
Guyana Revenue Authority under the Tax Act, Chapter 80:01** (s.18 urban, s.20 rural; issued under
s.71 via GRA's **Licence Revenue Office**) [V].

The Municipal and District Councils Act Cap 28:01 is the *local government* statute. **The council
does not issue the business's trade licence.** What the municipality or NDC actually does is:
rates and taxes on the property (Georgetown commercial property is rated at **275% of assessed
value** [V]); the **Sanitary Certificate**; food handler and food premises certification; and
stamping building plans alongside CH&PA.

**An owner who "went to City Hall for a trade licence" has gone to the wrong building.** Correct
this early — it wastes weeks.

### The standard prerequisite chain [V]

This is the sequence, and it is the thing most worth explaining to a client up front:

> Business Registration (DCRA) → TIN (GRA) → **approved building plan (CH&PA *and* M&CC/NDC)** →
> **Fire Certificate (Guyana Fire Service)** → **Sanitary Certificate (M&CC or NDC)** →
> [Food Handler's Certificate if food] → [Drug Certificate from the **Poison Board** if OTC drugs] →
> **GRA trade licence** → [liquor certificate from the District Licensing Board → liquor licence
> from GRA]

**The building plan, fire certificate and sanitary certificate are the binding bottleneck** for most
SMEs — see the provisional licence concession below, which exists precisely because they routinely
take longer than the licence cycle.

### Trade licence — GRA, Tax Act Cap 80:01

**Who needs one — s.18(1) [V]:** anyone occupying a store, shop, room, shed, yard, **stall**, office
or wharf **in or within one-quarter mile of Georgetown's municipal boundaries, or in New Amsterdam,
Linden, or the townships of Anna Regina, Bartica, Corriverton, Lethem, Rose Hall, Mabaruma or
Mahdia**, where goods are sold or exposed for sale. Spirits, drugs and patent medicines need their
own separate licences.

**Fees are charged on floor area, annually [V]:**

| Urban (s.18) | Fee | Rural (s.20) | Fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≤150 sq ft | G$1,000 | ≤120 sq ft | G$500 |
| 150–300 | G$2,000 | 120–2,000 | G$2,000 |
| 300–1,000 | G$4,000 | >2,000 | G$5,000 |
| 1,000–2,500 | G$8,000 | | |
| **2,500–5,000** | **G$18,000** | | |
| 5,000–10,000 | G$25,000 | | |
| 10,000–15,000 | G$50,000 | | |
| 15,000–20,000 | G$75,000 | | |
| >20,000 | G$80,000 | | |

**The bands jump steeply — 2,500 sq ft costs G$8,000; 2,501 sq ft costs G$18,000.** Area is
calculated by the **Licence Revenue Officer**, who must communicate the calculation (s.18(2)), and
**the applicant may appeal the measurement to the Magistrate within seven days**, whose decision is
final (s.18(3)–(4)) [V]. **For a business near a band boundary, a disputed measurement is worth
appealing.**

**Statutory exemptions worth knowing [V]:** no licence for a **cabinet maker** on premises ≤150 sq ft
(s.18 proviso (i)) · **no licence fee** where premises are used *solely* to sell firewood, charcoal,
fresh milk, butter, cheese, bread, biscuits or cakes (proviso (ii)) · in rural districts, no licence
at all for quinine, thymol, bread, ice, sweetmeats, jam, jellies, fresh milk, butter, cheese,
firewood or charcoal (s.20 proviso (a)). **None of these authorises selling uncooked fresh meat** —
that needs a butcher's licence (s.25).

**Renewal and payment — this catches people out [V]:**
- Licences run for **one year beginning 1 January** (s.62(1))
- Duty is **due 1 January and must be paid on or before the last day of February** (s.63(1))
- Where duty is ≥G$6 it may be paid in **two moieties** — first by end February, second by 31 July.
  **Failure to pay the first moiety on time makes the whole amount immediately payable**
- **s.70: unpaid duties are recoverable by parate execution with interest at 45% per annum** [V].
  This is severe — flag it

**Requirements [V]:** application form · TIN and valid ID · **approved plan/letter stamped by CH&PA
and Guyana Fire Service** · Business Registration · **Fire Safety Certificate** · **Sanitary
Certificate (M&CC or NDC)** · **Drug Certificate from the Poison Board** if selling OTC drugs.
Renewal is lighter: form, previous year's licence, ID, business registration, sanitary certificate.

**⚠ The provisional licence — the most useful concession to know [V]**

GRA Licence Revenue Policy #3, under the Tax Amendment Act 2018:
- For **grocery shops, variety stores and snackettes not exceeding 15,000 sq ft**
- For businesses that **cannot yet produce building plans, fire or sanitary certificates**
- Costs **50% of the annual licence fee**
- Valid **not exceeding two years, no extensions**
- **Cannot** be used to obtain any additional licence, including liquor
- Requires proof that application was made to housing, plus a Licence Revenue Officer interview

**This is the single most useful thing to tell a client stuck in the CH&PA or Fire Service queue.**

**Other GRA licence fees [V]:** Food Restaurant **G$1,125**/yr · Ball & Dance **G$3,500**/event ·
Gas station **G$35,000** Georgetown / **G$17,500** rural · Druggist's licence **G$5,000**/yr urban
(G$1,500 if in a council-owned market) · Goldsmith **G$10,000**/yr · Huckster on foot/cycle
**G$2,030**, beast of burden **G$7,250**, motor vehicle **G$36,350** · Auctioneer **G$23,500** ·
Copy of lost licence **G$850** · Customhouse Broker **G$15,000** + G$5,000 application.

**⚠ Two unresolved conflicts — verify before quoting [U]:**
1. GRA's gas station page states "Trade Licence: G$400 to G$62,275 (varies by square footage)" —
   which **conflicts with the statutory scale** above (G$500–G$80,000). Cannot be reconciled
2. A GRA Facebook post says restaurant licence fees are area-based; GRA's own website states a flat
   **G$1,125**

**GRA does not publish a consolidated trade licence fee schedule.** The online "Licence Checker" is
an application-status lookup limited to the last 20 days, **not** a fee calculator.

### Food and hospitality

**Food Handler's Certificate [V]** — Georgetown M&CC **Public Health Department**, Meat and Food
Hygiene Section. Required for **anyone handling food commercially, including street vendors**.
**Fee G$5,000** (raised from G$3,000 in 2018, stable since — corroborated). Applications are **in
person**; there is no online form. Medical certificates via Georgetown Public Hospital, with
**physical examination and lab tests every 6 months** [V].

**⚠ Validity period could not be established from a primary source [U].** M&CC states medical
examination every 6 months; GRA requires the certificate for annual restaurant licence renewal.
**Safe operating assumption: medical every 6 months, certificate treated as annual.**

**Food premises registration [V]** — all eating houses, restaurants, provision stores and cold
storages must register with the **Food and Hygiene section of the M&CC**; quarterly and monthly
inspection rosters. M&CC published fees by floor area: 401–5,000 sq ft **G$8/sq ft** · 5,001–10,000
**G$7/sq ft** · over 10,000 **G$6/sq ft**. **Cold storage G$30,000 base + G$3,000 per additional
cold room.**

**⚠ M&CC's published fee for the 100–400 sq ft band reads "G$2,000 per sq ft," which would mean
G$200,000–800,000 — wildly out of line with the G$8/sq ft band directly below it. This is almost
certainly a typo for a flat G$2,000. Confirm with M&CC before relying on it [U].**

**⚠ The biggest live uncertainty in the food space: the Food Safety Act 2019.** Act No. 19 of 2019,
gazetted 19 July 2019, established the **Guyana Food Safety Authority** with power to licence food
businesses, charge fees and suspend or revoke licences [V]. **But a USDA report indicates its
provisions "have not been fully implemented" [M].** Seven years after gazetting, **it is unclear
which regime actually binds a Georgetown restaurant today.** Tell the client to ask GFSA directly
whether food business licensing under the 2019 Act is yet in force for their category.

### Liquor licence — Intoxicating Liquor Licensing Act Cap 82:21

**Note the name — it is not the "Liquor Licensing Act."** Act 1 of 1929, amended through 22 of 2010.

**Two-stage process [V]:** a **certificate from the District Licensing Board** under s.20, *then* a
licence from GRA (s.4). Three districts — **Demerara** (sits Georgetown), **Berbice** (New
Amsterdam), **Essequibo** (Vergenoegen or Suddie). Each board is three magistrates; the GRA
Commissioner-General acts as clerk.

**⚠ The timing trap that costs clients a year [V]:**
- Boards hold **four general licensing meetings per year**, no later than 31 March, 30 June,
  30 September and 31 December (s.10(2))
- **Applications for RENEWAL are dealt with only at the LAST general licensing meeting of the year**
  (s.10(1))
- Applications must be served on the Commissioner **at least thirty days before** the meeting
  (s.13(1)(a))

**Missing a window costs a full quarter — or, for renewals, a full year.** Diary this for any client
with a liquor licence.

**Fees [V]:** **G$5,000 certificate fee** to the Board on service of the application (issue or
renewal) — **this is separate from the licence duty paid to GRA**, and secondary sources routinely
conflate them. GRA licence fees effective 1 Oct 2020: Hotel / Restaurant / Members' Club **G$7,500**
new · Off-Licence **G$3,000** new, **G$1,250** renewal.

**⚠ No published fee found** for spirit shop, tavern, night club, malt liquor and wine, railway
station/stelling, passenger steamer, occasional, coconut rum, or restaurant/parlour licences, nor
for hotel/restaurant/club **renewals**. Do not guess — call the Licence Revenue Office.

**Grounds for refusal — s.12 [V].** Unfit premises · applicant of bad character · applicant allowed
premises to become a nuisance within 5 years · premises cannot be kept under effective police
control · applicant under 18 · and — **the one most likely to defeat a new applicant in a dense
Georgetown location** — *"there is a sufficient number of premises already licensed to meet the
needs of the neighbourhood."* **That is a discretionary market-saturation test. Advise clients not
to sign a lease conditional on getting a licence.**

**s.12(4) — a concrete design constraint [V]:** premises are **not fit** for a tavern or spirit shop
if they contain a dwelling or living room with internal communication to the part where liquor is
sold. **That part must be separated by a partition built up to the roof.** Catch this before fit-out.

**Members' clubs** may additionally be refused where the club has **fewer than 25 members**, is not
conducted in good faith, or habitually admits non-members merely to obtain liquor (s.12(2)). The
secretary must give the **Commissioner of Police 21 days' notice** before applying (s.13A).

**Appeal from the board's decision lies to the Full Court** (ss.26–27) [V].

**Penalties [V]:** selling **rum** or other spirituous liquor without a licence — fine **not less
than G$5,000, not more than G$50,000** (s.44) · selling malt liquor or wine without a licence —
G$3,000–15,000 (s.43) · spirit shop selling >2 litres of rum to one person — 1st conviction
G$3,000–15,000; 2nd, **suspension up to 1 month**; **3rd, licence forfeited** (s.62(2)) · gaming on
licensed premises G$5,000 on the holder (s.60) · failure to produce licence on demand G$3,000
(s.63). **s.79 gives a power of arrest** for selling spirituous liquor without a licence.

**Two obligations that are easy to miss [V]:** **s.46** — a signboard over a conspicuous door with
the holder's name in **white letters on black, each letter not less than three inches**, plus
"Licensed Hotel" / "Licensed Restaurant" / "Licensed Spirit Shop" etc. Fines G$1,000 / G$3,000 /
G$6,000 for successive breaches. **s.47** — holders of hotel, restaurant, club, tavern and spirit
shop licences must keep **Form 9** (rum received) and **Form 10** (other spirituous liquor) on the
premises.

### Pharmacy and drug shops [V] — a clean checklist
- **Fire Certificate** (Guyana Fire Service)
- **Sanitary Certificate** (M&CC or RDC)
- Updated **Business Registration**
- **Drug Certificate** (Poison Control Board, Ministry of Health)
- **Licensed Pharmacist Certificate**

Governed by the Pharmacy Practitioners Act 2003, under the Pharmacy Council of Guyana.

### Import / export [V]

- **ASYCUDA World** eSAD filing, generally through a **licensed customs broker** (GRA maintains a

- **Import licences** for controlled goods only, issued by **MINTIC** — meat, fruit, beet sugar,

### Guyana National Bureau of Standards (GNBS)

**Statute: GNBS Act, Chapter 90:16** [V] — Act 11 of 1984, amended by 2 of 1997 and 6 of 1997,
consolidated L.R.O. 3/1998. **Not Cap 90:02** — that is a different chapter.

**⚠ Domain trap: `gnbs.gov.gy` does not exist** (DNS failure). **Use `gnbsgy.com`** — the current
live site with acts, forms and fees. `gnbsgy.org` now 404s on most paths but is still heavily
indexed and still printed as the contact address on trade.gov. Email remains info@gnbsgy.org.

**⚠ Correct this misconception: there is no compulsory certification scheme for local
manufacturers.** Both GNBS product certification and the "Made in Guyana" mark are **explicitly
voluntary** [V]. What *is* compulsory is (a) compliance with declared compulsory standards,
(b) annual registration, and (c) permits to sell certain goods.

**The 17 monitored product categories under compulsory standards [V]:** electrical and electronic
appliances · weighing and measuring devices · textiles · garments · footwear · safety helmets ·
safety matches · tyres · gas stoves · cigarettes · PVC pipes · furniture · toys and playthings ·
Christmas trees and decorative lighting · cellular phones · gold articles · electrical equipment and
fittings. Plus **31 compulsory national electrical standards** effective 1 Feb 2020 [M], and a
**rice** specification order (Order 21 of 2003) [V].

*(A 2016 DPI article listed 20 commodities including fertilisers, soap powder, water and electricity
meters and seat belts — none appear on the current list. The list has been revised; do not rely on
the 2016 categories [U].)*

**🔴 Mandatory annual registration and fees — from the official March 2025 form [V]:**

| Category | Fee |
|---|---|
| **Importers** — registration | **G$5,000** |
| **Importers** — per examination of each consignment | **G$5,500** |
| **Manufacturers** — annual registration & monitoring | **G$30,000** |

All fees non-refundable. *(A 2016 press figure of G$30,000 for both importers and manufacturers and
G$15,000 for dealers is ten years stale — use the 2025 form.)*

**Permits to sell — s.22 [V]:** where a compulsory standard has been declared, **no person shall
sell that commodity** except under a permit from the Director and unless it complies. Permit forms
currently published for **gold articles, cellular phones, tyres, furniture, fittings and equipment**.
**No published permit fees found.** Refusal may be appealed **in writing to the Minister within
fifteen days**; the Minister's decision is final (s.25(5)).

**Labelling [V/M]:** **GYS 9-2** governs pre-packaged goods (2017 edition adopted from CARICOM
CRS 55-2). Required content: product name and brand · manufacturer or distributor name and address ·
net contents · ingredient list where applicable · durability or expiry date · **all in English** ·
on the principal display panel. **Statutory backstop independent of the GYS numbering — Weights and
Measures Act s.18(1):** pre-packaged goods sold by weight or measurement must have the **net weight
or capacity marked on the container**.

**⚠ Genuinely unresolved [U]: whether the operative compulsory labelling standard is the 1994 edition
(GYS 9 / GYS 10, per the 1998 Order) or the 2017 CARICOM-aligned edition.** The numbering also shifts
between sources. **A client must confirm the current standard number and edition with GNBS before
printing labels** — this is a real cost if they get it wrong.

**Legal metrology — Weights and Measures Act Cap 90:15** [V] (Act 15 of 1981, commenced 1 Oct 1998).
**Metric-only since 1 January 2002** — the Imperial system ceased to have legal force.

- **s.12: the verification certificate is valid for one year** — the statutory cycle is **annual**
- **In practice GNBS runs verification twice yearly** [M] — for gas stations, January–March and
  July–September
- Instruments covered: weighing scales, **petrol and fuel pumps**, flow meters, moisture meters,
  electricity meters, speed guns, FPSO custody transfer meters, measuring rules
- On passing, GNBS affixes a verification seal; inaccurate pumps are adjusted and **security seals
  affixed to the adjusting mechanisms**

**Statutory fees — Tenth Schedule [V]:** each weight 25 kg–5 kg **G$150** · each weight 10 kg–500 g
**G$80** · each set of weights 1 kg or under **G$60** · **each weighing machine and steelyard G$60** ·
wooden and metal measures **G$100 each** · **each petrol pump G$1,000**.
**⚠ These are 1998-vintage statutory figures. No published GNBS price list confirms they are what is
actually charged in 2026 — confirm with GNBS Legal Metrology before budgeting [U].**

**Penalties — s.46 [V]:** three bands, up to **G$27,500 / 3 months**, **G$110,000 / 1 year**, or
**G$55,000 / 6 months**. The court may additionally order **forfeiture to the State** of the
commodities, and may order the person to **cease manufacture or sale forthwith** while the
contravention continues. Weights and Measures Act s.36 carries only **G$1,000 or six months** — a
1981 figure, never uprated; **the real deterrent there is seizure and forfeiture, not the fine**.

*(Note a drafting defect [V]: s.46 assigns penalty bands by reference to "section 39(a)…(j)", but the
offences are in **s.45** — s.39 is "Protection against claims". The cross-references were not
conformed in consolidation, so the offence-to-penalty mapping is textually broken. Worth flagging to
an attorney if a client is charged.)*

### Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)

**Statute: Environmental Protection Act, Cap. 20:05** [V] — Act 11 of 1996, **amended by Act No. 17
of 2005**.

**⚠ The most widely circulated copy online — the GGMC-hosted L.R.O. 3/1998 PDF — is pre-amendment
and wrong on two appeal deadlines. Anyone advising from the free 1998 PDF will miss an appeal.**

**Authorisation vs permit — the distinction is statutory [V].** "Environmental authorisation" is the
umbrella term (s.2) covering four instruments: the **environmental permit** (s.11/s.13, the EIA
track), the **prescribed process licence** (s.22), the **construction permit** (s.21(1)(a)) and the
**operation permit** (s.21(1)(b)). Administratively, EPA's eServices portal collapses all four into
one product called "Environmental Authorization (EA)". **Say "Environmental Authorisation" to EPA;
reserve "Environmental Permit" for the s.11 instrument.**

**Why EPA clearance gates everything else [V]:** **s.14(1)** — a public authority **shall not give
development consent** where an environmental authorisation is required unless it has been issued.
**s.5** — every other authority **"shall defer to the authority of the Agency."**

**Fourth Schedule — nine projects that go straight to the EIA track [V]:** hotels, guest houses or
inns **above ten rooms** · hydro-electric installations · roads, harbours and airfields · dams and
liquid storage · waste water or waste treatment installations · importing waste matter · release or
keeping of GMOs · **harvesting and utilisation of forest resources** · **extraction and conversion of
mineral resources**.

**⚠ Critical reading point: the Fourth Schedule is NOT the list of businesses needing authorisation.**
It is the list of projects that skip the screening argument. **The far wider net is s.11(1)'s second
limb — "or any other project which may significantly affect the environment" — plus ss.19/21/22.
That second limb is what catches most SMEs.**

**s.21(1) is the catch-all for small operators [V]:** no person shall construct, alter, extend or
replace any plant, structure, equipment or apparatus **that may discharge a contaminant** into the
natural environment except under a **construction permit**; nor alter a process or rate of production
with the result that a contaminant may be discharged, except under an **operation permit**.

**Which SMEs need one:**

| Sector | Basis | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Sawmills / logging | Fourth Schedule item 8; EPA publishes live permits | [V] |
| Quarries / mining | Fourth Schedule item 9 | [V] |
| **Poultry farms** | EPA guidance: **500 birds or more** triggers it | [M] — **verify the 500 figure** |
| Fuel / service stations | EPA oversees new, existing and renewal | [M] |
| Hotels / guest houses | **Above ten rooms** (Fourth Schedule). A 10-room-or-under guest house is outside the Schedule but still catchable under s.11(1) | [V] |
| Manufacturing / food processing | EPA permit archive; trade effluent caught by ss.19/21 | [M] |
| Car washes, auto and welding workshops, restaurants | **No published EPA category document found** | [U] |

**⚠ There is no published, consolidated EPA category list or threshold schedule for small
businesses** — verified absent across EPA downloads, programme areas and forms. **Do not tell a
client "your car wash needs an EA" or "your restaurant doesn't" on current evidence. EPA screens
case-by-case under s.11(2). Advise the client to submit a project summary and obtain a written
screening decision — that decision is their protection.**

A separate **short-term noise permit** ("application to operate a sound-making device") exists —
relevant to bars, events and construction [V].

**🔴 Fees are denominated in US$, not G$** [V] — schedule posted October 2025:

| Fee type | Small | Medium | Large | Extra Large |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Application — new EA | 50 | 50 | 50 | 50 |
| Application — EA renewal | 50 | 75 | 100 | 125 |
| **EA (permit) — C1** | **100** | **500** | **1,500** | **3,100** |
| EA — C2 | 175 | 800 | 2,000 | — |
| EA — C3 | 300 | 1,100 | 2,500 | — |
| Vary EA (substantive) | 100 | 125 | 150 | 175 |
| Transfer / surrender of EA | 50 | 50 | 50 | 50 |
| Operate a sound-making device | 20 flat | | | |

**C1–C3 indicate low to high environmental significance. The fee is per year; a permit may be issued
for up to five years.**

**⚠ No published G$ fee found — the schedule is US$ only.** Secondary sources claim payment is made
in GYD at the Scotiabank daily rate but this could not be confirmed. **Clients must confirm the
conversion basis with EPA [U].** **No published EIA review fee** — consistent with **s.11(12), under
which the developer bears all EIA costs directly**, including consultants and public hearings.
**The criteria assigning a project to Small/Medium/Large/Extra Large or C1/C2/C3 are not published.**

**Duration and renewal [M]:** an EA runs to a fixed date **not beyond five years**. To continue, apply
for a **new** EA **at least six months before expiry**; the expired EA continues in force until the
new one takes effect. Other holder obligations: keep monitoring records **3 years** · notify change
of name or ownership **within 30 days** · **annual return before 31 March** (failure is an offence,
fine G$30,000–80,000) · report non-compliance **within 24 hours**, written report within 72 hours ·
varying an authorisation without approval — fine **G$75,000–500,000 and 6 months**.

**🔴 Two appeal deadlines that catch people out [V]:**

| Appeal | Correct period | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| To the Environmental Assessment Board against an EIA exemption | **30 days** from publication | The free 1998 PDF still says **sixty** — Act 17/2005 reduced it |
| Notice of appeal to the Environmental Appeals Tribunal | **14 days** of service | Regulation 16(1) still says **28 days** — it predates the amendment and was never conformed. **The Act prevails: file within 14 days** |

Other statutory periods [V]: public submissions after notice of project **28 days** · public
submissions after EIA and EIS submitted **60 days** · EIA available for public inspection for the
project duration **+ 5 years** · notice before EPA cancels or suspends **at least 7 days** · appeal
from Tribunal to Court of Appeal on a **question of law only, 21 days**.

**Penalties — Fifth Schedule [V].** Eight bands from **G$10,000–50,000** up to **G$800,000–2,000,000
+ 5 years**. **Body corporate multiplier: a company is liable to twice the prescribed minimum and
maximum fine, and twice any term of imprisonment.**

**Operating without authorisation — the specific exposure [V]:** **s.15(1)** — commencing a project
without an environmental permit attracts **paragraph (d): G$70,000–300,000 + 3 months.** For a
company, **G$140,000–600,000 + 6 months.** Carrying on a prescribed process without a licence is
**paragraph (f): G$300,000–750,000 + 1 year.**

**⚠ But the fines are 1996 figures, never uprated, and are now trivial in real terms. The operative
deterrent for an SME is s.15(2) — where a project has started without a permit, EPA "shall
immediately serve an order in writing directing him to immediately stop the project." That is a stop
order, not a negotiation — combined with the s.14 bar preventing any other agency granting
development consent.** That is what to warn clients about, not the fine.
